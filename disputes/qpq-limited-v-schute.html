Champerty & maintenance

QPQ Limited v. Schute

High Court of Ireland · Ireland · 2026 · Decided; disclosure ordered

Citation/Docket: [2026] IEHC 463

Parties & Funders

Posture: Defendant vs. funded corporate plaintiff, seeking disclosure of who was paying
Funder(s) involved: (funder identity confidential; identifying the funder was the relief sought)

Background

A Dublin fintech company sued a minority shareholder over an alleged breach of a shareholders agreement. It was common ground the company could not afford the litigation. It said its only funding came from its Swiss parent, which is lawful in Ireland. The defendant pointed to discovery documents including an email offering to share 50 percent of damages with those who fund the litigation.

Holding & Outcome

The High Court confirmed it has inherent jurisdiction to order disclosure of funding arrangements where there is a credible basis to believe a case is supported by unlawful third-party funding, since maintenance and champerty remain both torts and crimes in Ireland. Funding from a shareholder or creditor with a genuine interest needs no disclosure, but funding from anyone else may be probed.

Practical Lesson

Ireland is not England. If your matter touches the Irish courts, funding from anyone other than a shareholder or creditor is legally dangerous, and your casual messages about it are discoverable.
