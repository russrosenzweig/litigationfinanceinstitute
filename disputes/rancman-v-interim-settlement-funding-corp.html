Consumer litigation funding

Rancman v. Interim Settlement Funding Corp.

Supreme Court of Ohio · US (OH) · 2003 · Decided; agreements void. Later superseded by Ohio statute regulating consumer legal funding

Citation/Docket: 99 Ohio St.3d 121, 2003-Ohio-2721, 789 N.E.2d 217

Parties & Funders

Posture: Consumer vs. pre-settlement funders, seeking to void the advances
Funder(s) involved: Interim Settlement Funding Corp. and Future Settlement Funding Corp.

Background

Roberta Rancman, injured in a car accident, took a $6,000 advance in exchange for the first $16,800 of any recovery within twelve months, rising to $27,600 within two years. The court of appeals had held the advances were unlicensed loans.

Holding & Outcome

The Ohio Supreme Court skipped the usury question and voided the advances outright as champerty and maintenance, holding that a contract making repayment contingent on the outcome of a pending case gives a non-party an impermissible interest in the suit, impedes settlement, and promotes speculation. Its line that a lawsuit is not an investment vehicle became the most quoted sentence in American anti-funding case law.

Practical Lesson

This is the high-water mark of the old rule, and the Ohio legislature promptly overrode it by statute. It explains why the entire consumer funding industry now writes strictly non-recourse contracts and lobbies state by state.
